Viavo is a voice assistant. To do what you ask, it has to hear you, understand you and reach the apps and data on your phone. This page says exactly what leaves your phone, where it goes, how long we keep it and how to delete it. We do not sell your data and we show no advertising inside the app. We do advertise Viavo elsewhere, and section 9 says how we measure that.

1. Who is responsible

Viavo is developed and operated by its developer (“we”). Contact us through the Support page.

2. Your account

You sign in with Apple or Google. We receive the identifier that provider gives us, your e-mail address (with Sign in with Apple it may be a private relay address) and, if provided, your name. The account keeps your settings and data together across your devices and lets us apply fair usage limits. Sign-in is handled by Supabase Auth. With Sign in with Apple we also keep the token needed to revoke the sign-in at Apple when you delete the account.

3. Your voice

Recording starts when you tap the microphone button on the phone or on the CarPlay screen, or when you start Viavo from its widget, Control Center, the lock screen or the Action button. It also starts by itself, so you can answer without tapping: right after Viavo asks you something, and when Viavo opens on the CarPlay screen (after its greeting, if there is one). Each time, a short tone plays as the microphone opens (unless you turn Sounds off in Settings) and the screen shows that Viavo is listening. Recording stops when you finish speaking, after 6 seconds if nobody speaks, and after 30 seconds at most. There is no wake word and no listening in the background.

When the microphone opened by itself and caught words that were not meant for Viavo (passengers talking, the radio), Viavo stays silent. Those words are still turned into text and read by the assistant to tell that they were not a request, but they are not kept: not in your conversation history, not in memory and not in our records, which only note that such a turn happened and how many words it had.

  • Server recognition (default). The recording is streamed over an encrypted connection to ElevenLabs’ speech-to-text service with a short-lived token issued by our server, and the text comes back to the app. To recognise names right, the stream carries a short list of hints: the names of your contacts, the artists in your library and the streets and towns of your recent routes. For your first requests, for some languages, when the stream fails or when it is turned off on our side, the recording is uploaded to our server instead and transcribed by two services at once, OpenAI and ElevenLabs, so a word one of them mishears can be checked against the other. Both get the same list of hints, and OpenAI also gets the languages you are likely to speak (those of your earlier requests and of the app). We do not keep the audio after the transcript is produced.
  • On-device recognition. In Settings you can switch to recognition on the phone. The app then downloads a speech model (from Hugging Face, about 500 MB) and your audio never leaves your phone; only the text does.

4. Understanding and answering

The text of your request is sent to our server and from there to Anthropic’s Claude model, which works out what you want and writes the answer. Before that, the request text alone also goes to TypeSafe AI’s decision model, which only says whether the request is about the weather, your calendar, a call or new chat messages, so that the answer can be looked up in one step; it writes nothing and gets no other data. For questions about current facts, the model may run a web search; your request text is what is searched for. When you ask for a place by its quality (a good restaurant, a well-rated salon, whether a shop is open now), the search text goes to Google Places together with the city you named or, for places around you, your coordinates. When you ask for a place on the way somewhere, the destination you named (or the coordinates of your saved home or work) and your coordinates also go to Google’s Routes API, which works out the drive so that places can be ranked by the detour they cost. When you ask where you are (which street you are on), your coordinates and the language of your request go to Google’s Geocoding API, which returns the address at that point for the answer. The answer is turned into speech by Microsoft Azure Speech (or ElevenLabs or OpenAI when it is unavailable); only the answer text is sent to them.

To answer well, each request carries some context from your phone:

  • your location (coordinates from the phone’s location service), time zone and app language, and the home and work addresses you saved in Settings, so the assistant can find places near you and route you home. Coordinates are used to answer the request; after it, the server keeps only the area the request came from, rounded to about a kilometre, with the city name as the phone gives it, and the home and work you saved, rounded the same way, for the notifications of section 5 (never a street address); a bug report keeps only the city and country. Separately, our server notes the country each request comes from: the country alone, as the phone’s location service names it (or, failing that, worked out from the request’s network address), never the coordinates (section 5). The phone also reports how fast and in which direction it is moving, for questions about what you can see around you; of these, only the speed is stored, in the technical record of the request (section 5), to see how often Viavo is used while driving;
  • which navigator and music app you chose, which apps are installed and which permissions are off, so the assistant can say what it can and cannot do;
  • what is playing now, when you ask about music;
  • your upcoming calendar events and reminders, when the request concerns them.

Opening Viavo in the car. When you open Viavo on the car screen, the phone sends the same context without any words of yours, so the assistant can say one or two useful sentences before it listens: the drive to work in the morning or home in the evening, a calendar event starting soon, or a station you usually turn on in the car. For the drive, your coordinates and the coordinates of your saved home or work go to Google’s Routes API, which returns the time with today’s traffic. The station is found from the stations you asked Viavo for in the car over the last three weeks. The short reply is kept as part of the day’s conversation like any reply, so that “yes” to its question is understood; nothing is said and nothing is sent to the language model when there is nothing useful to say.

Messengers. If you connect Telegram or Slack in Settings and ask about your messages (“what did Anna write?”, “tell the team I’m late”), our server reads or sends, on your behalf and only at that moment, what you asked for, and passes the text of the fetched messages to the language model so it can answer. Voice messages are transcribed by OpenAI. The access to your account (the Telegram session, the Slack token) is encrypted with a key kept only on the server and stored on your phone, never on the server; the fetched messages are not stored. The assistant’s spoken retelling is kept as part of the day’s conversation like any reply. You agree to this on the Messengers screen before connecting, and “Disconnect” ends it. The mechanics are described in detail in How Viavo handles your messages.

Claude routines. If you add one of your own Claude Code routines in Settings (a job you set up in your Claude account at claude.ai) and ask the assistant to run it (“run create task in Claude with this message”), our server starts that routine once with the task you gave: your dictated words, or the exact text of the message you asked about, with its sender and chat. The routine then runs in your Claude account, on your subscription and with the connectors you gave it there, and is covered by Anthropic’s terms for your account, not by this policy. When it finishes it sends a short report back to our server, and the assistant reads it to you once. The routine’s access token is encrypted with a key kept only on the server and stored on your phone, never on the server; “Remove” on the Claude routines screen ends it.

Memory. When Memory is on (it is by default; the switch is in Settings → Memory), the assistant keeps facts you tell it about yourself (“remember that I drive an electric car”: your car, people, places, tastes) and what it notices in conversation, and daily summaries of your conversations so it can answer “what did we talk about on Monday?”. Facts are shown and deleted one by one on the Memory screen or by saying “forget about…”. To search the summaries, each one is turned into a numeric embedding by OpenAI; only the summary text is sent for that.

Weather questions send your coordinates to Open-Meteo. Podcast searches send the search text to Apple’s iTunes Search API. When your music app is Spotify, YouTube Music or Deezer, a music request sends the search text (the artist, song, album or playlist you named, not your words) from our server to that service’s catalog API, so the app opens on the right track; Apple Music searches on your phone, and Amazon Music and Yandex Music open their own search screen for the request (nothing is sent to Amazon or Yandex from our server). A request for a live radio station sends the station’s name and your Apple Music country from our server to Apple’s music.apple.com catalog search, so Apple Music plays the right station. The station’s name also goes to the station search of Simple Radio (Streema), of TuneIn when it is your radio app, and, in the countries where it is available, of iHeartRadio together with your country, so the station opens in the radio app on your phone. A request for a YouTube video sends the search text, or the channel name, to Google’s YouTube Data API. None of these searches is tied to your account with that service, and we do not store them. Places are looked up with Apple Maps on your phone under Apple’s own privacy terms; when Apple Maps does not know the destination of a route or the address you ask to save as home or work, that destination or address, your approximate coordinates and the language of your phone go from our server to Google Places, and the place it finds is used instead. We count such lookups without keeping what was looked up.

6. Permissions on your phone

Viavo asks for each permission when you first need it and works without any of them except the microphone. Microphone: to hear you. Location: for routes, places and weather. Contacts: to call by name. Apple Music: to play from your library and play live radio. Calendar and Reminders: to create and read events and reminders. HomeKit: to control accessories. Notifications: tips about what Viavo can do, news, and replies to your requests, now and then and never at night; each topic can be switched off in Viavo Settings → Notifications. Tracking (the “Allow Viavo to track your activity” dialog): only to measure our advertising, see section 9; Viavo works the same whichever way you answer. You can change any permission in Viavo Settings → Permissions or in the iPhone Settings app.

7. Apps Viavo opens

When you ask for a route, music or a video, Viavo opens the app you chose (Apple Maps, Google Maps, Waze, Yandex Maps or Navigator, Apple Music, Spotify, YouTube Music, Deezer, Amazon Music, Yandex Music, Apple Podcasts, YouTube) and passes it the destination, the item found or the search text. When you ask Viavo to open an app by its name (a navigator, a messenger, a taxi or delivery app, the Calendar…), it opens that app’s main screen and passes it nothing; when the app is not installed, Viavo may open its App Store page instead. From that point the other app’s privacy policy applies.

9. Measuring our advertising

We advertise Viavo on Meta’s platforms (Facebook, Instagram) and in Apple Search Ads. To know which ads bring drivers who stay and which do not, so that the money goes to the ads that work, three things happen.

  • Meta’s SDK in the app tells Meta that the app was installed and opened, together with an anonymous install id, the device model and the iOS version, and, if the SDK itself crashes, a report about its own code that is not tied to you. It also tells Meta, once each, when you first reach a few steps of your first days: finishing the setup, the first request Viavo carried out, the first action on the phone (a route, a message, music), the first request with CarPlay connected, three requests on the first day, coming back the next day, tapping the subscribe button, and connecting Telegram or Slack. Only the name of the step goes, with the kind of action, the plan or the messenger where one applies, so that our ads can be tuned for drivers who actually use the app. Never your voice, your requests or anything you say, type or read.
  • Purchases. When you start a subscription or buy a credit pack, RevenueCat (our purchase provider) and Meta’s SDK tell Meta that a purchase happened on that install, with the same anonymous id and the price, so Meta can tell which ad led to it. Your name, e-mail and account are not sent.
  • The tracking dialog. iOS asks once whether Viavo may “track your activity across other companies’ apps and websites”. If you allow it, the phone’s advertising identifier (IDFA) is added to what Meta and RevenueCat receive above, which lets Meta match the install to the ad more precisely. If you do not allow it, nothing else changes: Apple still reports to Meta, in aggregate and with a delay, that an ad led to an install (SKAdNetwork / AdAttributionKit), without any identifier of you. You can change the answer at any time in iPhone Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking.

For Apple Search Ads, the app reads an attribution token from Apple and passes it to RevenueCat, which asks Apple whether the install came from one of our campaigns. The token identifies the ad click, not you. Meta and Apple keep what they receive under their own privacy policies; our account with them holds campaign figures, never a list of people. Which countries our drivers come from is counted on our own server, from the country of their requests (section 5); we send none of this count to Meta or Apple. We never buy audiences from, or share your data with, data brokers.

Visits to this website. viavo.app counts its visits on our own server (Supabase, EU) without cookies: the page and its language, the website you came from (its address only), campaign tags in the link, whether the App Store button was tapped, your time zone and the country it points to, the browser language and the type of device (phone, tablet or computer). A random visit number lives only in the open browser tab, so pages of one visit count together; nothing identifies you across visits. The IP address is not stored, and the records are deleted after 400 days. Nothing about these visits goes to Meta, Apple or anyone else.

10. Deleting your data

Signing out removes the session from the phone. To delete your account and everything in section 5, use “Delete account” in Viavo Settings, or ask us via the Support page and we will do it within 30 days. Contact and music data is replaced on each sync and removed with the account.

11. Your rights

If you are in the EU, the UK or another place with data protection laws, you can ask for a copy of your data, its correction or deletion, and object to processing. Our legal bases are performing the service you asked for and, for phone permissions and for tracking, your consent, which you can withdraw at any time; and for measuring our advertising without an identifier, our legitimate interest in knowing which ads work. Write to us via the Support page.

12. Children

Viavo is for drivers and is not directed to children under 16. We do not knowingly collect data from children.

13. Changes

When this policy changes we update the date at the top. Significant changes are announced in the app.
