How Viavo handles your messages

Message and email connections. Updated 8 November 2026. This page explains the mechanics; the Privacy Policy is the binding document.

Telegram and Slack are read when you ask; their fetched source messages are not retained on Viavo’s server after the request is processed. The assistant’s retelling enters conversation history. Email and notification automations work differently, as explained separately below.

WhatsApp availability, 8 November 2026. WhatsApp notification reading and separately enabled background sending are implemented for the upcoming Viavo 1.0.11 on iOS 27 or later. The current public App Store version, 1.0.9, does not include the native setup. Signed setup templates are available to compatible builds; the app checks device support before offering them. SMS/iMessage and Viber do not have the same released setup. Email reading is prepared for a new app release and is being verified.

1. Telegram and Slack: account access

When you connect Telegram (phone number, the code Telegram sends, your two-step password if you have one) or Slack (the usual “Allow” page in your workspace), our server receives the session once, at that moment. It encrypts it with a key that exists only in the server’s secret store, hands the encrypted blob back to your phone and forgets it. There is no table of accounts in our database. If Telegram asks you to link a login email first, the address you type goes through our server to Telegram and is not kept either.

Your phone keeps the blob in the iOS Keychain and sends it along with every request you make. The server decrypts it in memory for the few seconds the request takes and drops it again. So:

  • a copy of our database contains nothing that opens your Telegram or Slack;
  • your phone alone, without the server’s key, holds nothing usable either;
  • the access is per phone: a new phone means connecting again, and nothing follows you around.

In Telegram, Viavo appears in Settings → Devices as a signed-in device, and you can end it from there at any time. In Slack, it is listed under your workspace’s apps. “Disconnect” in Viavo removes the blob on the phone and requests sign-out from the provider. If the provider is unavailable, end the session in its settings.

2. Telegram and Slack: reading and sending

  1. You say, for example, “what did Anna write?”. Your words become text (by ElevenLabs’ speech recognition, or on the phone if you chose on-device recognition).
  2. The language model (Anthropic’s Claude) decides what is needed: find the chat named Anna, read its unread messages.
  3. Our server connects to Telegram or Slack as you, through their official interfaces, the way a Telegram app on a second device would, and fetches only that: the list of your chats to find the right one, and the messages of that one chat. “What’s new?” fetches the chats with unread messages and the last few messages in each; a search fetches the matches. Nothing is downloaded in bulk and nothing is kept.
  4. The text of those messages is given to the language model so it can tell you what they say. For a voice message, the audio is downloaded, turned into text by OpenAI’s speech recognition, and the audio is discarded.
  5. The reply is spoken to you (Microsoft Azure Speech turns the reply text into speech). The connection to the messenger is closed as soon as the reply is composed.

Sending works the same way in reverse. Viavo sends only the message you dictated, to the one chat you named, and by default it first reads the recipient and the text back and waits for your “yes”. You can switch that confirmation off per account in Settings. It never sends to several chats at once and never adds anything you did not say.

Viavo does not mark chats as read behind your back. A chat is marked read only when you ask, or when you turn on “Mark as read” for that account.

3. Telegram and Slack: storage

Not retained on Viavo’s server after the requestKept, like any conversation with Viavo
The messages fetched from the messenger. The audio of voice messages. Your session or token (the server never stores it). The list of your chats and contacts in the messenger. The text of what you asked and the text of the assistant’s spoken reply. When the assistant retells a message to you, that retelling is part of its reply and is kept for 7 days as the day’s conversation, as described in the Privacy Policy (section 5), so you can follow up with “reply to her”. Daily summaries and the long-term memory are built from those replies; memory can be turned off and any fact deleted in Settings → Memory.

Our technical logs record the type of request, how long it took, how many accounts were connected and whether a message was sent. They do not contain message text, chat names or contact names.

5. Telegram and Slack: controls

  • The consent switch on the Messengers screen: off means nothing is connected and nothing is sent.
  • Confirm before sending, per account: Viavo reads the message back and asks before sending.
  • Mark as read, per account: off by default.
  • Disconnect: removes access from the phone and requests sign-out at Telegram or Slack. You can also end the session from inside Telegram or Slack.
  • Delete account in Viavo Settings removes everything we keep for you.

6. Email: reading on request

In a version that offers this feature, after consenting to text processing you can connect a personal Gmail, iCloud or Yahoo account with an app password. Only supported addresses at those providers and certificate-verified encrypted IMAP connections are accepted. Custom or work servers, access to the entire Mail app, email sending and attachments are not supported.

The address and app password pass through our server during connection. The server encrypts the credentials; the encrypted blob stays in your phone’s Keychain and accompanies a request. The server uses credentials in memory and does not keep them in its database. Disconnecting removes the blob from the phone but does not revoke the provider’s app password: revoke it in your email account’s settings.

When you ask, the server finds unread or recent mail, optionally by sender or subject, and fetches up to five messages per request. Long text is bounded and truncation is reported; attachments and external HTML resources are not downloaded. Reading leaves unread status unchanged by default. If you separately enable marking mail as read, it applies to successfully fetched messages; it does not confirm that the phone has finished speaking their text.

Our server, the language model and the speech service listed in section 4 process the selected text. The email provider receives login details and reading requests; its copies of your mail remain with it. Email text and its spoken retelling do not enter Viavo’s persisted conversation history, daily summaries, diagnostic samples or analytics templates. Instead of that turn’s text, we keep a service marker, bounded message-selection identifiers and safe error information; an encrypted cursor for the next page expires after 30 minutes. No retention by Viavo does not mean no processing by the listed providers.

7. WhatsApp: notification reading and separate sending setup

WhatsApp receiving and sending are separate opt-in connections. Receiving requires consent to background transfer and storage; ordinary Telegram or Slack consent is not enough. The setup described here requires the compatible version and iOS listed above.

  1. While parked, open WhatsApp in Viavo Settings or during onboarding, then tap Connect.
  2. Add the receiving shortcut supplied by Viavo. In Shortcuts, tap Edit, select its trigger and enable Automation. Run it once and allow the requested access.
  3. If you want background sending, choose Set up sending separately. Add that shortcut, enable its automation and run it once to grant the requested permissions. Setup automatically groups the installed shortcuts in the Viavo folder; opening a shortcut alone does not activate it.

Ask Viavo to read notifications received after setup, or dictate a private reply. Sending is limited to unambiguously identified senders whose notifications Viavo has received on the current installation. A phone contact alone does not establish a recipient for background sending, and group messages are not supported. Viavo asks you to confirm the recipient and text. Delivery is not guaranteed. Phone drafts remain a fallback: open the prepared message and tap Send when it is safe to use your phone.

An iPhone automation you configure can forward only new notifications available to it: source, sender information, title, preview text, time and service identifiers. It does not open entire chat histories, attachments or a reliable read status. Previews can be truncated or hidden by phone settings; if no notification arrives or the automation does not run, Viavo receives no message. The phone keeps events awaiting upload in protected app storage: at most 100 events and 2 MB, dropping events older than 7 days during queue maintenance. The access credential stays in Keychain; the server stores its hash and a connection record with your consent.

Events containing preview text are stored by Supabase in the EU (Ireland) and available for reading for 7 days after notification capture. When you ask, selected text goes to the language model and the reply to the speech service; that retelling enters the normal 7-day conversation history, its summaries and, with Memory on, remembered facts. Reading covers only saved events and does not guarantee a complete chat search. Observed sender names and addresses are stored for recipient selection; cleanup removes them after 90 days without new events. Recipient names you confirm are removed with the associated sender or connection.

If sending is separately offered and configured, Viavo first asks you to confirm a specific recipient and exact text. This is a separate private send, not a reconstruction of the original chat or group from a notification. The server creates a job that the phone can retrieve once within 5 minutes. Recipient and text are cleared from the server on the first retrieval, cancellation or expiry processing; scheduled cleanup deletes the status record after 7 days. The phone passes the recipient and text to the selected messaging service through the configured automation. Completion of the phone action does not prove delivery or reading; without confirmation the outcome can remain unknown.

Expiry prevents further event reading or job retrieval. Scheduled cleanup physically deletes expired events, old senders and jobs; it is configured hourly where the database scheduler is enabled and may run after expiry. Pausing stops intake and clears the channel’s phone queue but does not delete events already stored on the server. Disconnecting deletes the connection and its events, senders, recipient names and jobs. Previously composed conversation history is managed separately under the Privacy Policy. You can also disable the automation in Shortcuts.
